The instrument

A frame drum
with rings inside

The doira is the frame drum of Uzbek and Tajik music. It is taught from notation, but much of how it is played passes from teacher to student by ear.

Nine strokes

Chanted as ta (main hand) and ka (secondary hand). Bums have no syllable. Tap any stroke to hear a real recording.

Three questions, in the order a listener answers them without thinking: which stroke, where in the cycle, exactly when.

1

Level 1

Which stroke

A classifier trained on 197 isolated strokes reached 95% on held-out clips (91.7% in cross-validation), but only 48% on 164 strokes inside real phrases.

Alone, a stroke was easy to name. Inside a phrase, the same model needed something more.

2

Level 2

Where in the cycle

A doira rhythm is an usul: a cycle that comes back around. A model that ignored the usul cycle kept at most 28% of generated cycles intact. A cycle-aware generator kept 100%.

Level 3

Exactly when

In the to'y rhythm (a wedding usul, played during the yor-yor), the two baks in each group are played about 30–40 ms closer together than the other strokes. The player doesn't feel it. It fades at slow tempos.

Illustration at one tempo, using 35 ms (the middle of the measured 30–40 ms). Dashed lines are the even grid.

Listening test
